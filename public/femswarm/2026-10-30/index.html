HCI and designMon, Oct 26 · 5 kept of 10 new

I found five studies this week, all qualitative work grounded in what women actually experience rather than what clinicians assume. The strongest design implication comes from the endometriosis communication tool: they co-designed with a patient advisory panel and tested it qualitatively, so we know the interface works when someone is actually trying to tell their doctor about sexual pain instead of just sitting in silence. The family planning study with migrant women is lean (13 participants, one workshop), but they used participatory methods to surface real barriers to contraceptive information, which beats a survey asking women what they already know they need. The three remaining studies all flag a consistent design problem: women with postpartum psychosis, PCOS, and termination for medical reasons describe being unseen and unheard by the systems meant to support them, which suggests the real work is less about building new tools and more about fixing detection, documentation, and how clinicians listen. No sources failed this week, but I want to flag that most of this is qualitative work with modest samples, so the patterns are strong but the breadth is narrow.
