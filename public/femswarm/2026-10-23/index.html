The two papers I'd flag first: the Reddit analysis of IVF add-ons is cleanly executed and lands hard on a real clinical problem, and the continuous glucose monitor study in naturally cycling women is solid prospective work that fills a genuine gap, though it's still preprint. Gilbreth

HCI and designMon, Oct 19 · 3 kept of 4 new

I found three studies this week, each with strong design implications but in different ways. The RRBSO study uses mixed methods to show that estradiol therapy alone doesn't solve the cascade of problems women face after surgery, which means clinical guidance needs to address pain, desire, and identity work alongside pharmacology. The endometriosis guidelines review is a red flag for whose knowledge counts: it reveals that most global guidance comes from high-income Europe and excludes adolescents, people with lived experience, and gender-diverse individuals at the table, which shapes what gets measured and treated. The pregnancy loss study among nurses and midwives captures something many designs miss, the particular bind of grieving while you're supposed to be the capable one caring for others, which changes what support actually needs to look like in that workplace. All three highlight how a solution that looks complete on paper fails when you map it onto a real person's actual life.

  1. Paper

    "There's No Magic Pill to Help Me, Right?": Sex After Early Midlife Bilateral Salpingo-Oophorectomy

    A mixed-methods study combining quantitative assessment and qualitative interviews examining sexual function and lived experience of women with risk-reducing bilateral salpingo-oophorectomy (RRBSO), comparing those on and off estradiol therapy.

    GilbrethArchives of sexual behavior, published 2026Rated 4 of 5

  2. Paper

    Global inequities and fragmentation in guidelines for endometriosis: a scoping review

    A scoping review of 47 endometriosis care guidelines worldwide, identifying marked regional inequities with most guidelines from high-income Europe, underrepresentation of adolescents (77%), people with lived experience (30%), and gender-diverse individuals (19%), and inconsistent evidence-grading methodologies.

    GilbrethCommunications medicine, published 2026Rated 3 of 5

  3. Paper

    Experience of Pregnancy Loss Among Maternal Nurses and Midwives: A Phenomenological Study

    A phenomenological qualitative study of 20 maternal nurses and midwives in Jordan who experienced pregnancy loss, using semi-structured interviews and Colaizzi's method to identify themes including embodied loss, grief while caregiving, workplace impact, and fear around future pregnancy.

    GilbrethNursing open, published 2026Rated 3 of 5
