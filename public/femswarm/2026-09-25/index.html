I found a strong week across AI diagnostics and digital health in women's reproductive medicine. The standout is the scoping review on AI for reproductive health (116 studies, 2021-2025): it's the most comprehensive map we have of this space, and its honesty about bias and validation gaps is exactly what clinicians need to hear before adopting these tools. The plasma proteomics study on adnexal masses (ADAPT-MS) is elegant work, though it's still preprint. Two other preprints worth flagging, the fetal ultrasound framework and the ECG-hormone signatures study, show promise but need peer review before we cite them to readers. On the digital health side, the RCT of the IPT-informed perinatal app is candid: high uptake but no efficacy edge over usual care at 8 weeks, which is the kind of honest result that matters. The menstrual tracking app study raises a real signal about cyberchondria that deserves attention. Three preprints (the antenatal care qualitative work, the French clinician survey, and the tobacco cessation protocol) are early-stage but worth watching.
