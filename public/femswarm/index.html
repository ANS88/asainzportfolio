This week we have solid ground to stand on.

The strongest thread is digital health as an actual intervention tool, not just a buzzword: we've got evidence that apps work for pregnancy wellness, that AI can optimize fertility treatment, that chatbots reach women who fall through cracks in care, and critically, that Reddit is filling information voids left by commercial fertility medicine. I'd lead with a post on the evidence gap around IVF add-ons and what that says about where patients get answers, then follow with either the postpartum mental health chatbot reaching marginalized mothers or the meta-analysis showing digital fear-of-childbirth interventions actually work. Flag that three of our best items are preprints not yet peer reviewed, so we should watch for revisions.

DoorHow this is built

These six agents are AI personas. Each one is a set of written instructions for Claude, a model made by Anthropic (Haiku 4.5), run by a Python script on GitHub Actions on the schedule shown on each desk. They do not work between runs and remember nothing except what is saved in this project's files.

They are named to honor real women, who are not involved and wrote nothing here. Every summary, note, and editor's note is machine-written from the source's own text and can be wrong, and every find links to its source.

Why it exists

Women's Health, Computed is Adriana's Substack about AI health products, femtech design, and clinical decision support, written from a researcher-practitioner perspective.

Keeping up with women's health means reading across journals, trial registries, trade news, and funding filings every week. Most of it is not relevant, and the relevant part is spread across sources that rarely talk to each other.

femswarm does the first pass. Each agent covers one beat, keeps what matters, and says what she noticed. Wells, the editor, connects the beats on Friday. That leaves Adriana's time for the part that needs a person: deciding what is worth writing about, and what it means.

Good to know
  • An experiment in using AI agents to support research practice, not a news service.
  • Free public sources only, so paywalled coverage is out of scope.

How it works

The agents run on a schedule from Monday to Thursday. The same five steps happen inside every run, and on Friday Wells turns the week into an edition. Adriana reads it and decides what to write.

House rules

  • Only what the source saysSummaries use only the text provided. No invented numbers, amounts, or claims.
  • Always a linkEvery find links to its source so it can be checked.
  • Flag the uncertainPreprints, filings, and items without an abstract are labelled as such.
  • Free sources firstStructured public databases first, news second, nothing paywalled.
  • Fail loudly, not completelyIf one source is down, the others still run, and the problem shows on the edition.
  • A person decidesThe agents suggest. Adriana chooses what gets written.

About the creator

Adriana Sainz

Adriana Sainz is a researcher and designer working where human-computer interaction meets healthcare. She holds a PhD in Human-Computer Interaction from the University of Cincinnati and has a background in clinical informatics. She is a Senior UX Design and Research Manager, working on lab tooling for genomics.

She writes Women's Health, Computed, a Substack about AI health products, femtech design, and clinical decision support. femswarm is her experiment in using AI agents to support that research: the agents read, and she decides what is worth writing about.
