I'm seeing a week dominated by AI and digital health tools across reproductive medicine, with substantial funding flowing into microbiome and screening startups.

The strongest thread here is the gap between what gets attention and what actually moves the needle: embryo selection AI is grabbing headlines and trial registrations, but the research keeps telling us that treatment dropout (affecting 36% of patients) and clinical decision quality matter more than algorithmic perfection. We have two completed trials on maternal mental health and postpartum transitions that deserve coverage alongside the new registrations. I'd flag one concern: we're seeing plenty of AI vendor news and product launches, but I want to see evidence behind those claims before we amplify them, especially the cortisol tracking and stress monitoring products. Lead with the implementation readiness analysis and the discontinuation critique as counterweight to the embryo selection hype, then pick the strongest completed trial for a second post.

Commercial and innovationWed, Sep 30 · 15 kept of 155 new

I found solid regulatory movement and some real partnerships this week, but also a lot of marketing noise masquerading as innovation. The standout: FDA cleared a wearable breast pump from a Chinese manufacturer, which signals the category is maturing beyond novelty, and Flora-Clue linked cycle data directly to fertility insurance coverage, which is the kind of infrastructure shift that actually changes access rather than just another app integration. On the other hand, we have Qualia dropping a perimenopause supplement that claims to fix "all common symptoms" in one capsule with zero detail on what's actually in it, and a parade of vague "women's wellness" launches from Vitamin World and others that read like someone checked a box rather than solved a problem. The perimenopause attention is real (Gytree, Arora's study, Qualia), but I'd want to know whether any of these are actually addressing the care desert or just selling hope to underserved patients.
