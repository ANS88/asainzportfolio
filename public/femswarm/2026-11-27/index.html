I'm looking at a week heavy on AI and digital health across reproductive life stages, which is exactly where the money and research attention are flowing right now.

The strongest thread is algorithmic prediction in pregnancy: we have longitudinal models for adverse outcomes, preterm birth forecasting, postpartum depression risk, and urinary incontinence, plus solid evidence that health information systems improve early detection and referral. There's real clinical potential here if these tools actually reach the patients who need them most. I'd pitch Adriana two posts: one on what these prediction models can actually do (with hard questions about validation and who benefits), and one on the implementation gap, since the Portuguese postpartum depression protocol and the HCI work on fragmented care systems both hint that building the tool is easier than getting it into clinics where it matters. Fair warning: two preprints in this batch haven't been peer reviewed yet, and that IVF AI dosing study found no real difference between the tool and conventional practice, which deserves a skeptical read before we promise too much.

HCI and designTue, Nov 24 · 7 kept of 12 new

I found solid work this week, though the picture is uneven. The two strongest pieces for design are the narrative threat zine-ing study and the postpartum AI preprint, both CHI work that show how women navigate fragmented systems through storytelling and sense-making rather than compliance checklists. That shifts what we should ask from a tool. The Be Well, Mommy feasibility study reaches only 15 mothers and 5 providers in one region, so it's early stage, but it matters that they're measuring real barriers to help-seeking, not just whether women download it. The peer interviewer methods paper is elegant and practical, showing that research design itself is an access point. I flagged that the postpartum AI piece hasn't been peer reviewed yet, so take the framing with appropriate caution. The visual storytelling essay and the IVF support protocol are valuable for centering dignity and emotion, though the latter is still a protocol, not findings. Mobile health in India rounds out the picture but speaks to a different context. No major failures, just gaps in what we can say about implementation and who stays engaged over time.
